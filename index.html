Welcome!

I'm a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, where I develop machine learning frameworks to understand complex biological systems at the intersection of computational biology, cheminformatics, and drug discovery.

My research at U-M, mentored by Dr. Sriram Chandrasekaran, focuses on building data-driven approaches to elucidate antifungal drug-pair synergy, leveraging protein sequence and drug structural embeddings across various fungal pathogens.

I completed my PhD at Louisiana State University under the guidance of Dr. Michal Brylinski. My doctoral research focused on data-driven approaches to elucidate bacteria–metabolite interactions in the human gut microbiome, leveraging enzyme encodings and metabolite structural embeddings across various mammalian gut bacterial species. A significant part of this work involved designing data analysis approaches and graph neural network (GNN) models to predict gut bacterial metabolism and synergistic anticancer drug combinations, enhanced by novel data augmentation strategies. I also built Biopython-based data curation and cleaning pipelines for projects on ligand-binding site classification.

My academic journey also includes a BS-MS dual degree in Chemistry from the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research Bhopal, where I developed cheminformatics tools for toxicity prediction and biofilm-inhibitory molecule classification, and an MS in Chemistry from The Ohio State University, where I worked on deep neural network–based prediction of Newtonian forces on dipeptide atoms calculated from MD trajectories.

I've also worked as a Research Associate at the Aquatic Germplasm and Genetic Resources Center, where I developed high-performance image classification tools using YOLOv10b, achieving 96% accuracy in distinguishing fertilized embryos from unfertilized eggs in Xenopus laevis. Currently, I am exploring LLM-based approaches for binding affinity prediction and antibiotic resistance modeling in microbes.

I'm always open to discussing computational biology, new research, or potential collaborations. Feel free to explore my Publications or connect with me on LinkedIn.
